# License Guide

This guide explains the VECTRA license in plain language.

The formal license is provided in:

```text
LICENSE
```

If this guide conflicts with the formal license, the formal license controls.

## License Name

```text
Yixuan Huang Non-Commercial Attribution Template License Version 1.0
```

## Is VECTRA Open Source?

VECTRA is source-available, but it is not distributed under a conventional
open-source license.

The source code is visible and available for personal use, but redistribution,
republication, derivative template distribution, and commercial exploitation are
restricted.

## Why a Custom License?

Common licenses such as LPPL, MIT, Apache, GPL, or Creative Commons
ShareAlike-style licenses do not match the intended use policy.

VECTRA allows users to create personal CVs and resumes, but it does not allow
users to republish the template, create a competing derivative template, or
redistribute modified template source files.

## What You May Do

You may:

- Download or clone VECTRA from the official repository.
- Edit your own profile and CV content.
- Compile your own CV or resume.
- Use your generated PDF for personal academic and professional purposes.
- Submit your generated PDF for job, internship, fellowship, graduate school, or
  research applications.
- Upload your generated CV or resume PDF to your personal website.
- Share your generated PDF without visible attribution.
- Recommend VECTRA by linking to the official repository.

## What You May Not Do

You may not:

- Redistribute the VECTRA template source.
- Repost the repository elsewhere.
- Mirror the repository as a template.
- Publish a modified version of the template.
- Create another CV or resume template based on VECTRA.
- Include VECTRA in a template bundle or template gallery.
- Sell VECTRA or a modified version of VECTRA.
- Use VECTRA in a paid CV, resume, recruiting, consulting, or document
  preparation service without permission.
- Remove copyright or license notices from the template source.

## Generated CVs and Resumes

Your generated CV or resume belongs to you.

You may:

- Submit it
- Print it
- Email it
- Upload it to your website
- Share it with advisors, employers, collaborators, or admissions committees

You do not need to include visible attribution in the generated PDF.

The generated document is different from the template source.
